Understanding HEPA Filtration Technology

HEPA stands for High Efficiency Particulate Air. These filters remove 99.97 percent of particles 0.3 microns or larger from air passing through them. This level of filtration captures bacteria, viruses, and many chemical particles. HEPA filters represent the gold standard for air filtration in healthcare and biohazard cleanup. Louisville properties benefit significantly from HEPA-filtered air scrubbing.

HEPA filters work through mechanical filtration rather than electrical charge. Air passes through densely woven fiberglass matting that captures particles. The filter's design creates multiple pathways that force particles to collide with filter fibers. Most particles are captured on first contact, while smaller particles are eventually trapped. This passive process continues working as long as air flows through the filter.

Negative Pressure Environments Explained

Negative air pressure means more air is being removed from a space than enters it. This pressure differential pulls air into the space from surrounding areas. Air can only escape through the filtration system. Nothing unfiltered can escape the containment area. Louisville biohazard teams create negative pressure to guarantee complete air isolation.

Negative pressure is measured in inches of water column, a precise measurement of pressure differential. Professional equipment monitors and maintains correct negative pressure throughout cleanup. Pressure that's too low fails to contain air properly. Pressure that's too high creates discomfort and potential structural issues. Louisville professionals maintain optimal pressure using calibrated monitoring equipment.
