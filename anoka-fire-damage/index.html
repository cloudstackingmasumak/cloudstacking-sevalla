A loss like this in Anoka leaves more behind than most people expect.

A fire is over in minutes, but the damage keeps working long after the flames are out. Smoke settles into walls and vents. Soot eats at metal and paint. Water used to put the fire out soaks into floors and drywall. What happens in the first hours often decides whether a room gets cleaned or gutted.

This guide walks through the first steps to take after a fire, what professional restoration actually involves, and how to choose the right local crew.

Choosing a local pro

Look for a crew that answers the phone any hour, since fire damage does not wait for business hours. Ask if their technicians are IICRC certified, which is the main standard for restoration work.

Ask whether they handle the full job, from board-up to the final rebuild, so you are not managing three different companies. Ask for a clear written scope before work starts, so you know what is being cleaned, what is being replaced, and roughly how long it will take.

Costs and timeline

Cost depends on how much of the home the fire and smoke reached, how much water went in, and how much rebuild is needed. A single smoke-damaged room is a small job. A fire that spread through the attic and several rooms is a large one.

Timelines run the same way. Cleanup and drying can take a few days. A full rebuild can take weeks. A good crew gives you a realistic timeline up front instead of a guess.

Get help now

If you are dealing with fire or smoke damage, the fastest way to limit the loss is to get a certified crew on site quickly. Local help is available any hour, so you do not have to wait until morning to start.

What to do first

Stay out of the home until the fire department says it is safe. Structures can be weak, and the air inside can still be unsafe to breathe after a fire.

Call your insurance company and open a claim. Take photos and video of every room before anyone moves or cleans anything. Keep receipts for a hotel, food, and anything you buy while you are out.

Do not wipe soot off walls yourself. Wiping the wrong way grinds soot in and turns a cleanable surface into one that has to be repainted or replaced. Do not turn the HVAC back on, since it can push smoke and soot through the whole house.

Get a professional crew on site fast. Soot is acidic, and every day it sits it does more permanent damage to metal, stone, and finishes.
