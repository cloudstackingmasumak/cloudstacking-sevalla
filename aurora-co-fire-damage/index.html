Practical guidance for Aurora property owners about checklist after a house fire, safety, professional cleanup, and the recovery process.

The shock of experiencing a house fire leaves homeowners disoriented and unsure what to do first. Taking the right steps immediately after a fire prevents additional damage and protects your insurance claim. In Aurora, the combination of fire damage and subtropical humidity creates urgency to act within hours, not days. This checklist walks you through the essential actions in the correct order, from immediate safety to professional restoration. Following these steps protects your family, your property, and your financial recovery. The first few hours determine whether secondary damage like mold growth becomes a major problem. Each step builds on previous ones, so skipping steps or changing the order creates complications later.

Immediate Safety and Access

Before you touch anything, confirm that your Aurora home is safe to enter. The fire department will usually perform a walkthrough after extinguishing flames and identify any ongoing hazards like unstable structures, hidden embers, or gas leaks. Ask firefighters for a written clearance if possible. Do not rely on your own judgment about whether a building is safe. Firefighters have the training and experience to spot dangers that homeowners miss.

Once you have clearance, move through your home slowly. Watch for weak flooring, falling debris, or sharp metal edges created by the fire. Wear protective gear including gloves, a respirator mask, and closed-toe shoes. If you smell gas or see structural damage that concerns you, leave immediately and call the fire marshal's office before re-entering. Your Aurora home can be restored. Your safety cannot.

Keep documentation of all work completed. Receipts, contracts, permits, and inspection reports should all be saved. These records prove that work was done properly and to code. They are valuable if you ever sell the home or need to verify that restoration was completed correctly.

Write down what you have lost or damaged. List rooms, major items, and approximate value. This list helps your insurance adjuster understand the scope of loss. If you have receipts, warranties, or proof of purchase for valuable items, gather them. Original purchases prove value and speed up claim settlement.

Safety First: Protecting Your Family and Yourself

Before you do anything else, confirm that everyone in your household is accounted for and safe. Meet with your family outside the home and do a headcount. If anyone is injured, call emergency services or head directly to the nearest hospital. In Aurora, Sumner Regional Medical Center is the primary acute-care facility, so make a mental note of your closest urgent-care or ER option.

Do not re-enter your home until a firefighter or fire marshal gives the all-clear. Aurora Fire Department will conduct a safety inspection and may post notices on the structure. If the fire department says the building is unsafe, listen to that guidance. Smoke and soot can hide structural problems, and weakened floors or ceilings can collapse without warning. Your life is more valuable than any possession left inside.

Gather Documents and Inventory Belongings

If tarp coverage is needed for roof damage, ask the crew to provide before and after photos of their work. These photos document the emergency measures taken and support insurance coverage for board-up costs. Keep all receipts and invoices from emergency board-up services for your claim file.

Request that your restoration company photograph the home's exterior condition after board-up is complete. This documentation shows the extent of damage that required emergency measures. Insurance companies expect this level of documentation for emergency repairs.

Contact your utility companies (electric, gas, water, internet) about the fire. Let them know that the home may be unsafe for extended periods while restoration happens. If utilities are damaged and shut off, ask about restoration timelines. Understanding utility status helps restoration teams plan their work.

Arrange for utilities to remain available if safe. Your restoration team may need electricity and water to complete work. If utilities have been shut off due to damage, ask when they can be restored. Temporary utility connections may be needed during restoration work.

The First Hours: Immediate Safety and Evacuation

Before you can do anything else, make sure everyone is safe and away from the fire area. If the fire is still burning, call 911 immediately and get everyone outside. Do not try to fight a significant fire yourself. Let professional firefighters handle the emergency. Your job is to evacuate safely and account for all family members and pets.

Stay out of the home until firefighters give you permission to approach. The structure may be unsafe, electrical hazards may be present, and carbon monoxide levels may be dangerous. Take the fire department's word when they say the building is not safe for entry. Waiting a few hours is much safer than rushing in and getting injured.

Aurora, CO heat speeds up mold growth in wet environments. Mold colonies can become visible within 72 hours of the fire. Once mold establishes itself, remediation becomes far more expensive than water removal would have been. Professional crews use industrial dehumidifiers, air movers, and moisture detection equipment to dry a fire-damaged home properly. This is not a time for DIY solutions; professional equipment removes moisture that household fans and open windows cannot reach.

Create a Detailed Inventory of Lost Items

Plan for displacement lasting weeks or months if the fire was substantial. Structural repairs, HVAC restoration, and painting can take considerable time. Your insurance will support temporary housing for the duration of necessary repairs, so stay in communication about the projected move-back date.

Finally, once restoration is complete and you return home, take time to inspect the work before the claim is finalized. Walk through every room with the restoration company and your adjuster. Look for any spots that were missed, any unfinished work, or any issues that arose during restoration. Your thorough final inspection ensures you are satisfied with the recovery and can close the chapter on this difficult event in Aurora.

Contact your insurance company about temporary housing benefits before booking accommodations. Confirm what types of housing are covered and what the daily or weekly limit is. Some policies cover hotel stays, others cover rental apartments. Understanding your coverage helps you make appropriate choices.

Look for temporary housing close to your home if possible. You want to be near the restoration site to monitor progress and access any belongings or documents you forgot. Hotels in your Aurora area may have extended stay rates that work better than daily rates for longer fire recovery periods.

Contacting Your Insurance Provider

Request a claim number from your insurance company. Use this number on all communications, receipts, and documentation related to the fire loss. It becomes your reference point for every conversation with your adjuster. Do not accept a settlement offer from the adjuster before getting a second opinion on the damage assessment. Many homeowners qualify for higher settlements when they understand how to document and negotiate their claims properly.

Contact your mortgage company and renters insurance company if applicable. Mortgage companies have to be notified of fire damage because they have an insurable interest in the property. Notify them and provide them with your insurance information. Renters insurance might cover some of your personal belongings and temporary housing. Know what your policy covers.

Firefighters use enormous amounts of water to extinguish house fires. Gallons per minute flow through your home, soaking walls, floors, belongings, and sometimes basement or crawl spaces. This water damage often causes more long-term problems than the fire itself because moisture left in place grows mold within 24 to 48 hours. Beginning water removal immediately is not optional; it is essential.

Standing water must be pumped or extracted from floors, crawl spaces, and basements. Carpets need to be pulled up to allow floors underneath to dry. Drywall may need to be cut at the bottom to create air circulation and allow moisture trapped inside the walls to escape. Furniture pulled away from walls creates space for dehumidifiers and fans to circulate air. Every hour that water sits in your home increases the risk of mold and structural decay.
