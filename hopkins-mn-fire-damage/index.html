Understanding Soot Damage to Electronics

Soot particles are conductive and extremely small, which makes them particularly problematic for electronics. When soot settles on circuit boards, connections, and sensitive components, it can create electrical short circuits or prevent proper contact between components. The acidity of soot can also corrode metal connections and damage components over time.

Electrical appliances are particularly vulnerable to soot damage because the heat and particles can interfere with motor operation, heating elements, and control circuits. Televisions and computer equipment are damaged when soot blocks cooling vents or infiltrates internal components. Even appliances that appear to function after a Hopkins fire may have internal soot damage that eventually causes failure.

Soot contains compounds that are hygroscopic, meaning they absorb moisture from the air. This moisture combined with soot creates corrosion on metal components inside electronics. Over time, this corrosion expands and damages connections, eventually causing electrical failure. This delayed failure can occur weeks or months after a Hopkins fire if soot is not professionally removed.

Professional Electronics Cleaning and Restoration

Specialized electronics cleaning is different from general cleaning because it must remove soot without damaging delicate components or creating moisture damage.
