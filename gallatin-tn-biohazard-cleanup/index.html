The Immediate Health Risks You Face

Biohazard contamination exposes you to bloodborne pathogens when you handle cleanup yourself. Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, and HIV are pathogens commonly found in biohazard situations. These pathogens can survive in dried blood for extended periods. Simply touching contaminated surfaces without proper protection puts you at risk. Any cut or abrasion on your hands provides a path for pathogens to enter your body.

The risks multiply with improper technique. Aerosolizing contamination while scrubbing exposes your respiratory system to pathogens. Touching your face while contaminated creates mucosal membrane exposure. Sharing contaminated materials with family members spreads risk to people who had nothing to do with the original incident. These are not hypothetical risks. Occupational exposure to bloodborne pathogens happens to people in Gallatin, TN every year. Healthcare workers and cleanup professionals receive extensive training to prevent exposure. You lack this training and protection.

Bloodborne Pathogens and Exposure Risk

Bloodborne pathogen transmission is serious and potentially life-altering. Hepatitis B and C can cause chronic liver disease and cirrhosis. HIV leads to AIDS if untreated. All three require lifelong medical management. Infection happens through exposure of infected blood to your bloodstream through breaks in skin or mucous membranes. Small cuts or abrasions you do not notice can provide entry points. Dried blood that looks harmless still contains infectious pathogens.

The exposure risk is not just during cleanup. Incomplete cleanup that you do not realize occurred leaves your family exposed to pathogens for weeks or months afterward. Everyone who enters the space is at risk. Children who play in areas you thought were clean face exposure. Guests who visit are unknowingly exposed. This is why professional cleanup is not optional.
