Lakewood homeowners need to secure a fire-damaged property quickly to prevent further loss and stay within insurance requirements.

In the hours immediately after a fire is extinguished, one of the most urgent but often overlooked priorities is securing the property. Broken windows, damaged doors, and compromised walls leave a home vulnerable to weather, animals, and unauthorized entry, all of which can add significant additional damage on top of what the fire already caused.

Board-up services address this vulnerability quickly, using plywood and other materials to seal openings and protect the structure until permanent repairs can begin. For Lakewood homeowners dealing with the shock of a fire, this step often gets pushed aside in favor of more visible concerns, but delaying it can lead to real financial consequences.

This article covers why board-up and securing matter so much, what the process typically involves, and how it connects to both the physical protection of a home and the insurance claims process. Understanding this step helps Lakewood families move through the immediate aftermath of a fire with fewer surprises and less risk of compounding an already difficult situation.

Why Securing the Property Matters Immediately

A fire-damaged home is uniquely vulnerable in the days and weeks following the incident. Broken windows and damaged doors create obvious entry points, but even structural damage to walls or roofing can leave gaps that expose the interior to outside elements in ways that were never a concern before the fire.

Weather exposure is one of the most immediate risks. Rain, wind, and temperature swings can cause significant secondary damage to a structure that is already compromised, sometimes affecting areas of the home that survived the fire itself relatively unscathed. In Lakewood, even periods of light rain can cause meaningful damage to an exposed interior if openings are not properly sealed.

Unauthorized entry is another serious concern. An unsecured, visibly damaged home can attract trespassers, looters, or even unhoused individuals seeking shelter, all of which introduce safety risks and potential liability issues for the homeowner. Quickly securing the property removes this vulnerability and protects both the structure and anyone who might otherwise wander inside.

What Board-Up Services Typically Involve

Board-up work generally starts with an assessment of every opening in the structure that needs to be sealed, including broken windows, damaged doors, and any holes in walls or roofing caused by the fire or by firefighting efforts to access and extinguish the flames.

Plywood is the standard material used for board-up work, cut to size and secured over window and door openings in a way that withstands wind and provides a real barrier against entry. For larger structural openings, tarping may be used in combination with plywood, particularly for roof damage where a full board cannot be practically installed.

The installation itself needs to be done securely, not just propped in place. Properly fastened board-up work should hold up under normal wind conditions and resist casual attempts at removal. For a Lakewood home, this level of securing is what actually prevents the follow-on damage that an inadequately protected property would experience during even a single storm or windy week.

Roof Tarping After Fire Damage

Fires that affect the roof structure, whether from direct flame damage or from firefighters needing to ventilate the roof to fight the fire safely, often leave the home vulnerable to water intrusion from above. Roof tarping addresses this specific vulnerability separately from window and door board-up.

Heavy-duty tarps get secured over damaged roof sections, typically anchored with wood strips called batten boards that are nailed down along the tarp edges to prevent wind from lifting or tearing the material. This creates a temporary but effective barrier against rain until permanent roofing repairs can be completed.

Roof tarping requires careful attention to detail, since an improperly secured tarp can actually cause more damage by flapping in wind or allowing water to pool and eventually seep through small gaps. Lakewood's occasional heavy rain events make this an important detail to get right, particularly for homes with more significant roof damage that will take weeks to fully repair.

Timing: Why Speed Matters After a Fire

The window between a fire being extinguished and permanent damage occurring from exposure is often shorter than homeowners expect. Within just a day or two of rain exposure, materials that survived the fire relatively intact can develop water damage, and that timeline shrinks further with unauthorized entry risks.

Insurance policies generally expect homeowners to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a covered loss, which includes securing the property promptly. Delaying board-up services can create complications with a claim if additional damage occurs that could have been prevented with timely action.

For Lakewood homeowners, this means board-up should typically happen within the first day after a fire, often arranged through the same restoration company handling the broader recovery, or through an emergency board-up service if immediate availability differs between providers. Acting quickly here protects both the physical structure and the strength of the insurance claim.

Board-Up and Insurance Claim Documentation

Board-up costs are typically covered under most homeowners insurance policies as part of the effort to prevent additional loss after a covered event. However, proper documentation matters for reimbursement, so keeping receipts and, ideally, before-and-after photos of the board-up work supports this part of the claim.

Insurers may also want to see that the securing work was done to a reasonable standard, meaning a slapdash job that fails to actually protect the property might not fully satisfy this requirement even if some effort was made. This is one reason working with an established restoration or board-up company, rather than attempting a rushed DIY job, tends to hold up better during the claims process.

Lakewood homeowners should keep all invoices related to board-up and securing work in the same file as other fire-related documentation, since adjusters often ask for this information specifically when reviewing the overall timeline and cost breakdown of a claim.

Security Beyond Board-Up: Additional Measures

Beyond covering physical openings, some situations call for additional security measures depending on the extent of damage and the property's location. Temporary fencing around the perimeter can add another layer of protection, particularly for homes with extensive damage that will take a long time to repair.

Security lighting and, in some cases, temporary security patrols or monitoring systems provide additional deterrence against unauthorized entry, especially for properties that will remain vacant for an extended period during major reconstruction. These measures are more common for larger fires with significant structural damage rather than smaller, more contained incidents.

For a Lakewood homeowner assessing what level of security their situation calls for, the general guideline is matching the security measures to the length of time the property will remain vulnerable and the extent of the damage. A property expected to be under repair for several months warrants more robust security planning than one with a quick two-week repair timeline.

Balancing Board-Up With Salvage and Access Needs

While securing a property is important, it needs to be balanced against the practical reality that restoration crews, insurance adjusters, and sometimes the homeowners themselves need continued access to the property throughout the recovery process. Board-up work typically includes provisions for this, such as a securable door panel rather than a fully sealed opening.

Coordinating access between the board-up company, the restoration team, and the insurance adjuster helps avoid situations where the property is so thoroughly sealed that legitimate work gets delayed. Clear communication about who needs access and when prevents unnecessary friction during an already stressful time.

For Lakewood homeowners managing multiple contractors and service providers during recovery, keeping a simple log of who has access to the property and when can help avoid confusion, particularly if any question arises later about who was on-site during the securing and restoration process.

Removing Board-Up Once Repairs Progress

Board-up and tarping are temporary measures, and removing them happens in phases as permanent repairs progress. Windows get uncovered once new glass is installed, doors get unboarded once functional replacements are in place, and roof tarps come down once permanent roofing repairs are complete.

This transition should happen carefully, with each area confirmed as properly repaired and weatherproofed before its temporary protection is removed. Removing board-up prematurely, before the underlying repair is fully finished, reintroduces the same vulnerabilities the board-up was meant to prevent in the first place.

For a Lakewood property moving through the later stages of fire recovery, this gradual removal of board-up materials often serves as a visible marker of progress, room by room and section by section, as the home transitions from a secured but damaged structure back into a fully repaired, livable space.

Protecting the Property Through the Full Recovery

Board-up and securing work might seem like a minor detail compared to the larger scope of fire damage restoration, but it plays an outsized role in preventing additional loss during a vulnerable period. Skipping or delaying this step can turn a fire damage claim into a much larger, more complicated situation involving weather damage, security incidents, or insurance disputes.

For Lakewood homeowners navigating the aftermath of a fire, treating board-up as an immediate priority, right alongside safety checks and initial damage documentation, sets a stronger foundation for the entire recovery process. A properly secured property protects everything that happens afterward, from the structural repairs to the personal belongings still inside the home.

Taking this step seriously from the very first day, and keeping good documentation throughout, helps ensure that the only damage a Lakewood family has to recover from is the damage the fire itself caused, not additional loss that better securing could have prevented.
