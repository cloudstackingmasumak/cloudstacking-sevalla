Documentation You Need to Provide

Provide the adjuster with a detailed inventory of damaged items and contents. List what was damaged, the general condition of each item, and estimates of value if possible. You do not need exact values, but ballpark ranges help the adjuster understand the scope of contents damage.

If you have receipts or photos showing what you owned before the fire, this is extremely helpful. A dining room table photographed before the fire, now burned, is easier for the adjuster to evaluate than just taking your word that a table was there.

Walk through your Birmingham property with a notebook or recording device and document conditions before your adjuster visits. Describe damage in each room. Note what was saved and what was lost. If contractors have already begun work in Birmingham, explain what was done and why. Having this information organized in Birmingham lets your adjuster work efficiently and ensures you do not forget important details about your property damage.

The final settlement of your claim involves reconciling all approved work, verifying that restoration is complete, and receiving final payment from your insurance company. Request a detailed settlement statement from your insurance company showing all approved damages, coverage limits applied, deductibles, and the final payment amount. Review the settlement statement carefully to ensure all items discussed during the claim process are included. If you disagree with the settlement amount, discuss concerns with the adjuster and your insurance agent before accepting the settlement. Once you accept the settlement payment, your claim is typically closed, so ensure you're satisfied before accepting final payment. Some restoration work may not be completed at the time final settlement is offered, requiring agreement about how remaining work will be paid.

Documenting Ongoing Damage and Recovery

Be present when the adjuster inspects your Birmingham home. Bring your documentation. Point out damage the adjuster might miss, like smoke inside the HVAC system or creosote buildup in the chimney. Walk them through your list of damaged items. Answer their questions thoroughly.

Adjusters sometimes take multiple visits to assess complex fire damage in Birmingham. They may order specialized inspections for HVAC systems, electrical wiring, structural framing, or asbestos testing. Cooperate fully. These inspections protect you by identifying hidden damage that changes the claim value. A Birmingham adjuster who finds structural fire damage early means a larger settlement for repairs.

Deductibles apply to each claim. Your deductible is the amount you pay before insurance pays anything. Supplemental Claims Process

If you and the adjuster reach an impasse, most policies include appraisal clauses. This process lets you hire an appraiser to represent your interests while the insurance company hires their own. The two appraisers meet, review the evidence, and attempt to agree on the loss value. If they can't agree, they hire an umpire who helps reach a final decision. This appraisal process is part of your Birmingham fire insurance policy and provides a formal way to resolve disputes without litigation.
