Plywood and Fastening Techniques

Plywood is the primary material for securing broken windows and small openings. Professional teams do not simply nail plywood over broken windows. Proper securing uses bolts and lag screws that penetrate the window frame and into the building structure. This fastening method resists removal by intruders and endures wind and weather. Plywood is cut precisely to fit each opening, minimizing gaps. The material is usually painted or treated to resist weather damage during the securing period. For larger openings or long-term securing, permanent boards might be reinforced or metal panels might replace plywood. In Marana homes, plywood board-up typically appears professional while clearly indicating that the property is damaged and undergoing restoration.

Windows and doors get boarded with plywood sheets cut to fit and fastened securely. The plywood is often reinforced with bracing to resist wind load and prevent casual removal. Larger wall breaches demand structural assessment and temporary bracing to prevent collapse and allow safe access.
