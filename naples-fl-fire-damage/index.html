Restoration Work Authorization and Contractor Selection

Once your insurance company issues a settlement, you can authorize restoration work to proceed. In Naples, you are generally free to select any licensed contractor you prefer rather than using contractors suggested by your insurance company. Choose a restoration contractor with fire damage expertise, proper licensing, insurance, and verifiable experience. Providing Documentation and Proof of Loss

You will need to provide your insurance company with detailed documentation of everything that was damaged or destroyed. Photographs and videos from immediately after the fire are crucial. These visuals prove what was lost before any cleanup or restoration began. Professional fire damage restoration companies often provide detailed documentation that supports your claim.

Create a comprehensive inventory of damaged items. Include as much detail as you can: the type of item, approximate age, condition before the fire, and estimated value. For expensive items like jewelry, electronics, or collectibles, provide receipts if you have them. Even without receipts, good descriptions help the adjuster understand what was lost.

The adjuster assigned to your Naples fire damage claim will schedule a time to inspect your home. This inspection is critical. The adjuster takes their own photographs, measures damage, identifies repairs needed, and estimates the cost of restoration. They also review your policy to determine what is covered and what exclusions might apply. Be present during the entire inspection if possible. Walk through your home with the adjuster, pointing out all damage and answering questions. If something the adjuster says does not match your understanding of the damage, speak up immediately and provide your perspective.

Documenting Ongoing Losses and Expenses

Beyond structural damage and destroyed belongings, fire damage often creates additional expenses that insurance should cover. Document the cost of temporary housing, meals, and transportation if you cannot stay in your home while it is rebuilt. Keep receipts for emergency repairs like tarping or board-up services. If you must relocate your business, document lost income and relocation expenses. Naples residents often incur costs for storage of salvageable belongings, temporary services, and increased insurance costs while your property is being restored. Photographs and receipts create the paper trail that justifies these additional expenses to your adjuster. Some policies include coverage for additional living expenses for a defined period, while others require you to prove the expenses are reasonable and directly caused by the fire damage.

During the adjuster visit to your Naples property, provide complete access and answer all questions honestly. The adjuster will photograph damage, measure affected areas, and assess the scope of restoration needed in your Naples home. Be present during the inspection to point out damage the adjuster might miss and ask questions about what will be covered. Final Approval and Claim Closure

When restoration is complete, walk through your home with the contractor and make sure all work meets your satisfaction. Take new photographs showing the restored areas. If you are not satisfied with any aspect of the work, communicate this before you sign off on completion. Once you approve the work, the contractor will request final payment from the insurance company. This payment should cover any remaining balance on the estimate.

Your insurance company may send their adjuster for a final inspection to confirm the work was completed as outlined in the claim. Be present for this inspection if possible. The adjuster will verify that the restoration matches the estimates and that no additional damage has appeared. Assuming everything checks out, the insurer will close your claim. This typically happens within a few days of final approval.

Obtaining Proof of Loss and Submitting Additional Information

Your insurance company may request a formal proof of loss statement, which is a detailed accounting of all losses you are claiming. Proof of loss requires you to list each damaged or destroyed item with its value. This document must be accurate and complete. Provide supporting documentation for items you are claiming. This might include receipts, credit card statements, bank records, or photographs of items before they were damaged. If you cannot locate original documentation for an item, explain how you know what it cost. Witness statements can sometimes help prove what you owned or the condition of your home. For high-value items, obtain written estimates for replacement. For example, if your roof was damaged, get a contractor's estimate for roof replacement. These estimates help support the value of your claim.
